Your concept: tent the Duo like the StandBy alarm. The near half of the inner display is the tuner; the far half is pointed at the wall and the table, so it becomes a lamp: solid red, amber, or green, flooding the surface behind the phone the way StandBy's alarm washes a nightstand. You don't read the cents, you read the room. Slide across the strings bar to lock a target note, or leave it on auto.

Same lamp trick, different job: the far half pulses the wall on the downbeat so you get a visual click in your peripheral vision without staring at a screen. Nothing to tap, nothing to unlock. Tent it mid-song and the metronome carries on; lay it flat open and the chart is where you left it.

Where each feature lives

TunerTented: near half is the tuner, far half is a color lamp. Stand: corner dot, expands on a lone plucked string. Open: sidebar.
MetronomeEvery pose. Tent pose is visual-first: the wall pulses.
Charts and tabsStand and open poses. Chords, tab, and free-form chart views; auto-scroll tied to tempo.
Capo and transposeOne control, two numbers: shapes you play and key you hear.
LooperBar-range loop with slowdown, in the stand's bottom pane and on the tented near half as a pass counter.
SetlistsOpen pose sidebar. Shareable as a link, readable without the app.
Pencil inkOpen pose, anchored to bars so it survives transposition.
Split ViewAnything beside Prop; tempo can follow a video's playback rate.

Everything is local and free. The only network feature is the setlist share link, and it's a plain-text fallback so nobody is forced to install anything.
